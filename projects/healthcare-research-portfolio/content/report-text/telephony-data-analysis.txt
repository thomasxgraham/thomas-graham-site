Telephony Data Analysis We analyzed AI-generated call summaries from the Five9 CDS Dashboard to
understand why patients contact support when using our digital tools. Across
topics, the data reveals a consistent pattern: a security or identity step—such as
verification, MF
A
, or password confirmation—is triggered, and that step fails in a
way the patient does not understand or cannot complete.
Most calls relate to access failures, but those failures present differently
depending on where a patient is in their journey and how clearly the system
communicates what is happening. Some callers explicitly describe not receiving a
verification code, being prompted for MF
A to the wrong number
, or resetting a
password without success. Others describe the problem more generically as “
can’t
log in” or “account not working.
” Still others simply request help because they are
unable to proceed toward their intended goal.
These are not different problems. They are different symptoms of the same
access and identity system failing patients at different points in their health
journey
.
When a security or recovery step fails, the system does not clearly explain
whyaccess is blocked or what it needs from the patient next. Patients then guess at
recovery actions—retrying login, resetting passwords, or requesting additional
codes. These attempts often fail to resolve the issue and can compound it by
triggering lockouts or additional security challenges, at which point human
intervention becomes necessary
.
This explains the quantitative patterns in the data. Roughly a third of calls
explicitly reference verification or message delivery
, while an even larger portion
reference login or password recovery failures. These categories overlap because
they represent progression through the same access failure lifecycle, not separate
root causes.
