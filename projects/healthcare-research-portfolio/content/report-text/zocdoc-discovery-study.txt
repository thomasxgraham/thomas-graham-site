Findings -
Discovery Study on ZocDoc Experience Introduction This report presents the findings from a recent Discovery study aimed at understanding why patients are or are not scheduling
appointments using our digital services. The study focused on uncovering insights to improve the user experience and increase
the adoption of our online scheduling platform. Participants were observed interacting with ZocDoc, a third-party platform that
helps users find healthcare providers. The study involved comprehensive feedback from patients to identify friction points and
successes in the process.
Methodology Participants were asked a series of questions during discovery interviews to gather their experiences and opinions on various
aspects of using ZocDoc. The questions covered topics such as personal experiences searching for a primary care provider (PCP),
challenges faced, thoughts on the guided search experience, and preferences for features like voice input and AI assistance.
Key Findings First Impressions of ZocDoc Participants value:
•
Insurance Information
: The inclusion of insurance information boosts confidence in coverage and aids in selecting providers.
•
Variety of Doctor Options
: The availability of various specialists, not just primary care physicians (PCPs), enhances the
service
'
s appeal
. When irrelevant providers are included (i.e., searching for a Cardiologist and an OB/GYN provider
embedded within the search results), trust in the service quickly erodes, potentially leading to abandonment.
•
Search Page Clarity
: The straightforwardness of the search page, focusing on essential elements like insurance, location, and
doctor type, is appreciated.
Participants have concerns about:
•
Insurance Compatibility
: Hesitation regarding the service
'
s ability to filter doctors based on specific insurance plans.
•
Initial Search Confusion
: Potential confusion in the interface, with one participant mistakenly believing they were viewing
search results without initiating a search.
Sorting Search Results Participants find sorting functionality unclear and disorganized, with issues in ordering by location and specialty visibility:
•
Sorting Functionality is Unclear (2 mentions)
: Confusion and frustration due to unclear sorting functionality
.
•
Disorganized Experience Due to Titles and Practices (1 mention)
: Confusing titles and the mix of practices versus individuals.
•
Inability to Order by Location (1 mention)
: Need to sort search results by distance, but this option is unavailable or unclear
.
•
Mismatch in Search Results (1 mention)
: Frustration with search results showing primary care doctors instead of the
requested specialists.
•
Need for Prominent Specialty Information (1 mention)
: Desire for doctor specialties to be more prominently displayed.
Map Features of ZocDoc Participants find the map feature useful for logistics and traffic, but some prefer filtering by location:
•
Map Feature Aids Logistics (2 mentions)
: Helpful for assessing traffic conditions and narrowing down search results.
•
Preference for Location Filtering (1 mention)
: Preference for filtering by location over using the map, finding the order of
results confusing.
Ratings and Reviews Participants value detailed reviews and information for informed decisions, with a preference for recent reviews and availability
over high ratings:
•
Preference for Detailed Reviews (3 mentions)
: Preference for reading detailed reviews for a comprehensive understanding,
despite openness to AI-generated summaries.
•
Importance of Appointment Availability (2 mentions)
: Prioritization of appointment availability over high ratings when
choosing a doctor
.
•
Value of Comprehensive Information (2 mentions)
: Appreciation for detailed information like reviews, network status, and
board certifications.
•
Significance of Recent Reviews (2 mentions)
: Suggestion to show the age of reviews, as recent negative reviews may
indicate a decline in quality
.
•
Trust in Google Reviews (2 mentions)
: Value placed on Google reviews, checking both positive and negative feedback to
assess a doctor'
s trustworthiness.
Sponsored Listings in Search Results Participants express skepticism about sponsored listings, preferring clear labeling and relevant, high-quality results:
•
Skepticism Towards Sponsored Listings (3 mentions)
: Skepticism about the quality of sponsored listings, preferring to rely
on reviews and ratings.
•
Preference for Clear Labeling (3 mentions)
: Acceptance of sponsored listings if they are clearly marked, understanding the
need for revenue.
•
Irrelevance of Sponsored Results (2 mentions)
: Frustration with sponsored results often being irrelevant to specific search
needs.
•
Desire for Quality Control in Sponsorship (2 mentions)
: Suggestion for a cutoff for sponsored listings based on ratings to
ensure quality
.
Guided Search Participants have mixed feelings about the guided search process, with preferences for preliminary questions and simplicity:
•
Preference for Preliminary Questions (5 mentions)
: Appreciation for preliminary questions to narrow down search results,
valuing time-saving and specificity
.
•
Concerns About Guided Process Timing (4 mentions)
: Feeling that the guided process should be introduced earlier and not
as a roadblock when searching for providers.
•
Desire for Interface Options (3 mentions)
: Suggestion for multiple interface options, such as guided tours or AI assistance,
to cater to different preferences.
•
Skepticism About Customization (3 mentions)
: Doubt about the effectiveness of customization features, feeling they may
not yield expected results.
•
Preference for Simplicity and Efficiency (3 mentions)
: Value placed on simplicity
, speed, and efficiency in the user interface,
similar to the TurboTax approach.
•
Interest in AI Assistance (3 mentions)
: Openness to AI-generated summaries and assistance, especially as an optional
feature for mobile.
•
Specificity in Search Results (3 mentions)
: Appreciation for the specificity of search results, especially when looking for
specialists like dermatologists.
•
Concerns About Data Collection (2 mentions)
: Preference for current interfaces over guided conversations due to concerns
about data collection.
•
Preference for Direct Access to Providers (2 mentions)
: Desire for direct access to all providers for their insurance without
unnecessary steps.
Overall Impressions of ZocDoc Participants appreciate the modern design and profile access but are frustrated with search accuracy and provider availability:
•
Frustration with Search Results (3 mentions)
: Frustration with search results showing providers outside the specified area
and not meeting their needs.
•
Appreciation for Modern Design (2 mentions)
: Positive feedback on the website
'
s modern, well-designed, and easy-to-
navigate interface, with features like insurance filters and automatic refresh.
•
Preference for Profile Information (2 mentions)
: Value placed on the ability to view detailed profiles, including education
and training, which is lacking in some provider apps.
•
Navigation and Interface Issues (2 mentions)
: Frustration with tedious dropdown menus and the opening of multiple
windows, despite the simple interface.
Use of AI and Voice Input for Searching Participants are open to AI and voice features but have concerns about control, privacy
, and efficiency:
•
Concerns About Control and Privacy (4 mentions)
: Concerns about control, permissions, and privacy when using voice
input features.
•
Preference for Typing Over Voice (4 mentions)
: Preference for typing over voice input for control and familiarity
, especially
on desktops.
•
Voice Input as a Modern Feature (3 mentions)
: Belief that voice input and AI features make the platform feel modern and
innovative.
•
Voice Input on Mobile (2 mentions)
: Finding voice input easier on mobile apps compared to typing in smaller dialog boxes.
•
AI-Generated Summaries (2 mentions)
: Openness to AI-generated summaries but preference for reading full reviews for
comprehensive understanding.
•
Preference for Convenience (2 mentions)
: Value placed on convenience features like auto-populating information over
lengthy question processes.
•
Preference for Typing on Desktops (3 mentions)
: Preference for typing on desktops due to familiarity and simplicity
, despite
the availability of voice input options.
•
Voice Input on Mobile Apps (2 mentions)
: Finding voice input easier on mobile apps compared to typing, especially in
smaller dialog boxes.
•
Flexibility in Interaction Methods (3 mentions)
: Emphasis on the need for flexibility in how they interact with sites,
preferring both typing and voice options.
•
Voice Input Not Always Preferred (2 mentions)
: Feeling that voice input does not add value and may lead to biased
recommendations, describing it as 'gimmicky
'.
Cross-selling Participants are open to alternative options and non-specialists but prefer consent for changes and specialists for specific
conditions:
•
Openness to Alternative Options (2 mentions)
: Acceptance of being offered alternative options if initial preferences are
unavailable.
•
Preference for Specialist Care (2 mentions)
: Preference for specialists for specific conditions like hair loss, but comfort with
non-specialists for general issues.
Looking for Other Services While Searching for a Provider Participants prioritize finding a PCP and scheduling appointments based on specific needs and recommendations:
•
Participants prioritize finding a PCP (2 mentions)
: Focus on finding a primary care provider (PCP) for annual physicals and
consideration of additional services like pediatric care.
•
Appointments based on specific needs (2 mentions)
: Preference for setting up appointments based on specific needs, such
as annual checkups, and following additional suggestions from healthcare providers.
Feature Suggestions Participants suggest enhancing search with filters, voice commands, and visual aids, and improving user experience with insurance
and health record integration:
•
Enhance Search with Filters and Visuals (3 mentions)
: Suggestion for immediate filtering based on known info, visual
representation of ratings, and prioritizing insurance and top-rated doctors.
•
Integrate Insurance and Health Records (3 mentions)
: Recommendation for features like taking a picture of insurance cards,
syncing health records, and a guide for identifying insurance plans.
