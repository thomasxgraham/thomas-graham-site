# Direction Framework

## Draw the business

Represent the complete product system:

- acquisition channels or triggering events;
- registration, access, or setup;
- activation and onboarding;
- value-producing behaviors;
- observable customer states such as active, at-risk, dormant, and re-engaged;
- transitions and interventions;
- revenue, cost, risk, or mission outcomes;
- instrumentation points and evidence IDs.

The Cornell example connects acquisition to registration/download, onboarding, and behavioral lifecycle states. Adapt the structure to the product; do not copy labels mechanically.

## Vision and value proposition

State:

- target customer and important problem;
- promised outcome and why it matters;
- differentiated mechanism or advantage;
- evidence supporting the direction;
- boundaries that preserve focus.

## Objectives and key results

- Prioritize a small set of objectives for the planning horizon.
- Explain why deprioritized objectives will not be funded now.
- Define three to five measurable KRs per objective.
- Specify population, calculation, baseline, target, and time window where known.
- Keep KRs as outcomes or validated learning, not task completion.
- Mark unaccepted targets and missing baselines as proposed or unknown.

## Viability and adoption

Assess buyer versus user value, pricing/funding, operating and AI inference/review costs, channel, change burden, support, training, legal/regulatory constraints, integration, capability, and scalability.

## Cornell grounding

This framework synthesizes CTECH102 objective/KR activities and action plan, Thomas's business-flow image, and the user/buyer guidance in CTECH101.
